## 2026-09-25 — Halloween: a host toggle that dresses the room and the night outside, for everyone

**Decided (owner: "feel free to add more of a Halloween vibe", then "host toggle").** `GameState.theme` is `'classic'` or `'halloween'`, set by the host through `host:action` (`{action: 'theme'}`). It's patched to everyone, logged ("Alice dressed the room for Halloween"), saved with the table, and a save without it defaults to classic. The Host tab has a "Halloween night" switch at the top.

What it changes, all built once at load and shown or hidden, so switching is instant:
- **Outside:** the Halloween layer of the world outside (see "The world outside"). The moon turns orange and the sky purple; jack-o'-lanterns, the graveyard, ghosts, bats, low fog, the black cat and the witch appear.
- **Inside** (`client/src/three/HalloweenDecor.ts`):
  - carved pumpkins on the mantel, the hearth, every window sill and the TV cabinet;
  - a cauldron bubbling green on the hearth;
  - cobwebs in the ceiling corners and over the bookshelf;
  - twelve candles floating above head height, bobbing;
  - paper bats swinging on strings from the beams;
  - fairy lights in orange, violet and green.

  All of it is placed from the room model's own objects (mantel, hearth, sills, beams), so it follows the room if the room changes.

**No new lights.** The pumpkins, the brew and the candles glow through emissive surfaces and additive halos. Adding or removing a scene light changes the light count, which recompiles every material in the room, a visible hitch on toggle, and a permanent extra light would cost every frame for everyone.

**Why a host toggle rather than a date or a per-player setting:** the room is shared. Everyone should see the same room, and the one running the game decides the mood. It's a two-value theme rather than a boolean, so a later theme (winter) is just another value.

## 2026-09-25 — The world outside: four more windows onto a living 3D night, seen through the panes like real holes in the walls

**Decided (owner: "more windows and give a more alive feeling with actual stuff outside… beautiful insane visuals… also depth").**

**Windows:** four new windows, two on the east wall flanking the picture, and two on the west wall flanking the chimney, cottage-style. They're built in `room.blend` from the existing window's parts, stretched nine-slice style so the frame and mullion keep their thickness. Each has its own `Window_View_*` pane, and `RoomLoader` now finds every pane (`windowViews`). The painted canvas that used to sit on the one pane is gone.

**How you see out: a portal, not holes.** The outside is its own `THREE.Scene` (`client/src/three/outside/`). Each frame a pane is on screen:
- it's rendered from the player's own camera, with a 2 km far plane where the room stops at 100 m;
- the target is a multisampled half-float texture at 0.6× resolution, scissored to just the panes' screen rectangle;
- every pane's shader shows that texture at its own screen position, then adds old-glass touches: a faint warm reflection at grazing angles and a breath of condensation in the corners.

So the view has true perspective and parallax, and walking past a window slides the lamp post against the hills. The room's walls stay closed boxes and the Blender geometry is untouched.

Two alternatives were rejected:
- **Cutting real holes and building the world into the room scene** would mean surgery on every wall, and the outdoor scene would render even when no window shows.
- **A stencil pass** can't be ordered against three.js's transparent pass, so glows and sprites in the room would be painted over.

It holds 60 fps at 1600×900 with a big window up close, and skips entirely when no pane is in view.

**What's out there:**
- **Sky:** a shader dome with twinkling stars and a faint Milky Way, a moon with its halo, drifting cloud banks and the odd shooting star.
- **Land:** rolling land in exponential fog, so each layer hazes a little more (the depth). A lake under the moon with a glittering reflection path. A seeded pine forest, a fenced yard with autumn trees swaying, lamp posts pooling warm light, a well, a bench, hay bales and a scarecrow.
- **Distance:** a village on a hill whose windows go dark and light again, a windmill turning on the eastern hill, and two mountain ranges.
- **Life:** fireflies, and autumn leaves drifting past the glass. Warm light from the room's own windows falls on the ground outside.

The land's shape and every placement are pure, seeded functions (`terrain.ts`, unit-tested), so every player looks out on the same world, the yard is level with the floor, and the views north (the village) and east (the windmill) stay clear of trees.

**Halloween** is a second layer of the same world, built once and shown on demand:
- the moon turns orange and the sky purple with an ember horizon;
- jack-o'-lanterns line the path and fill a pumpkin patch, and the scarecrow wears one as its head;
- an iron-fenced graveyard west of the yard, with a mausoleum, dead trees and ghosts fading in and out;
- bats circle the moon and low fog drifts over the yard;
- a black cat blinks on the fence, and every so often a witch flies across the moon.

**Rejected:** third-party asset packs for the outside. At night the world reads as silhouettes and glows, so procedural low-poly shapes serve and weigh nothing. The room stays the only big download, now 8.96 MB, up from 8.86.

## 2026-09-25 — The soundboard's look: a walnut cabinet with brass-bezelled jewel buttons, engraved nameplates and a picture lamp

**Decided (owner: "the soundboard should match the visuals from the room").** The wall board was a flat black slab with pastel boxes, the one thing in the room that looked like UI. It is now furniture:
- a walnut cabinet with a moulded frame and brass corner caps, around an oxblood leather field with saddle stitching;
- round buttons with a brass backplate, a bezel ring and a clear-coated enamel dome. Each slot has its own jewel tone (`SOUNDBOARD_JEWELS`), still per slot and not per sound. A filled button glows softly in that color, and an empty one is smoky dark glass;
- an engraved brass nameplate under each button, and an engraved brass plaque on the top rail;
- a brass picture lamp above it.

Everything is built in three.js as before. The wood, leather and brass are drawn on seeded canvases, so every player sees the same board.

**Alive:** whenever anyone plays a sound, from the wall, the menu or as a TV clip, its button sinks in and flares, for everyone. The Sound tab's little board shows the same jewel colors, so the two read as one thing.

**Two constraints shaped it:**
- **The room has no environment map**, and a room-wide one would relight everything that was tuned without it. So the board's brass gets its own small reflection map, a PMREM of three.js's `RoomEnvironment`. Without it, metal renders near black under point lights.
- **The room already has about a dozen lights**, so the picture lamp adds none. Its pool of light is painted into the leather, with a glowing slot and a soft halo sprite, the same way the room's other glows are halos rather than bloom.

## 2026-09-25 — Host controls: lock the table, remove a player, what everyone else may do, clearing the table

**Decided (owner: "the host should have more control", details left to Claude).** A host-only **Host** tab in the session menu, driven by one event, `host:action` (`shared/src/host.ts`):
- **Lock the table** (`GameState.locked`): nobody new can join. Players already at the table can still reconnect, and the host reopening a saved table still works. The join screen says the table is locked before anyone tries. A reopened saved table starts unlocked, since last time's lock was about last time's group; the permissions carry over.
- **Remove a player** (the × in their Players row, with a confirm press): they leave as if they had left themselves, their connection stops speaking for them, and they land on the join screen with "The host removed you from this table." They are kept out while the table runs (an in-memory list, never saved or broadcast), so removing someone means something even without the lock. The host can't remove themselves.
- **Everyone else may** (`GameState.permissions`), with the host always exempt:
  - **draw on the map** (and erase);
  - **use sounds**: the soundboard, adding sounds, and starting clips on the TV.

  The server enforces both: drawing events from others are dropped, and sound actions are refused with a reason. The client stays in step: the toolbar says drawing is off, a press on the bare table no longer draws (dragging minis and rolling dice still work), the Sound tab greys out, and the wall board says why.
- **Lock the TV:** the existing `clipLocked`, mirrored here.
- **Clear the table:** the map's drawings, the whiteboard, all dice (the host's secret ones included) or all minis. Each needs a confirm press.

Every change leaves a line in the table log ("Only Alice can draw on the map now", "Bob was removed by Alice"), so nobody is surprised.

**Why these, and not more:** they cover what a game master actually needs mid-session: keeping strangers out, dealing with a disruptive player, stopping doodles or sound spam during a tense scene, and resetting between scenes. Muting and handing over the host role already existed. Per-player permissions were left out, because a whole table either may or may not, and per-player switches add UI weight for a rare need.

**Clearing the drawings repaints every table.** Strokes reach clients one by one (`drawing:*`), so a client's copy of the state never counts them. The clear goes out as a full snapshot, and the room repaints whenever a scene it shows has lost strokes. For the same reason the Drawings button shows no count.

## 2026-09-25 — Secret dice: the host's dice only they see — filtered out of everything the server sends anyone else

**Decided (owner: "the host should be able to spawn invisible dice only for himself").**
- **Making them:** the Dice tab has a host-only "Secret dice" switch; its dice are `Dice.hidden`. The server refuses a hidden spawn from anyone but the host.
- **Who sees them:** only their owner, including after a host handover, and their rolls' log lines carry `visibleTo`. A roll mixing open and secret dice logs two lines, one open and one secret.
- **For the owner** they show as ghostly violet glass with a SECRET tag.
- **For everyone else** a secret die doesn't exist: rolling it by guessing its id gives "Die not found", and dragging it is relayed to nobody. It goes when its owner leaves.

**Privacy is enforced where state leaves the server, not on screen.** Hiding a die in the renderer wouldn't do, because anyone can read what their browser receives. `server/src/privacy.ts`'s `viewFor(state, player)` drops what that player may not see, and every outgoing state goes through it:
- the join ack;
- all 18 action acks, via `own(result)`;
- full snapshots (`broadcastState`, per viewer);
- patches that carry `dice` or `log`, per viewer, only when something is actually private, so ordinary tables still get one room broadcast.

**Tests:**
- Bob's socket records every message it receives, and not even the secret die's id appears in any of them: acks, patches, a scene-change snapshot, or a drag.
- A late joiner doesn't get it either.
- A break-round, with the filter disabled, fails those tests.

## 2026-09-25 — Minis: a miniature of your own character on the table; minis and dice are dragged, and a die that's pressed but not moved rolls

**Decided (owner: "Mini of your character").**
- **What a mini is:** each player can put one mini on the table: a painted-miniature version of their own character, the same model posed from its idle animation and scaled to 15 cm. It stands on a base in their color with a brass rim and a soft contact shadow.
  - The base's top is an unlit disc in the player's exact color. Under the table's spotlight a lit base washed out to white from above, which made minis indistinguishable in the table view.
- **Where they're stored:** `GameState.minis` (playerId → point), in table units, the same space as drawings and pings, so minis sit correctly on any map. A player's mini goes when they leave.
- **Placing one:** from the Map tab ("Put it on the table" / "Take it off"); moving it is a drag on the table itself.
- **Dragging:** the table's pointer handler's "claim this click" became "claim a press": a press on a mini or die picks it up.
  - Moving more than 5 px makes it a drag: the thing lifts slightly and follows the pointer.
  - Letting a die go without moving it is a click, and it rolls, as before.
  - A press anywhere else draws, as before; a press on a mini you can't move does nothing, instead of scribbling under it.
  - A hand cursor shows what can be picked up. Dragged things stay inside the play surface's edge.
- **Who can move what:** your own mini and dice; the host can move anyone's. Anyone can still roll any die by clicking it, as before.
- **Sync:** `mini:move` / `dice:move` are fire-and-forget, like `player:move`.
  - Sent at most every 60 ms while dragging, with the final spot always sent on letting go (`throttle.ts`).
  - The server checks them, keeps them in state (late joiners, saved tables) and relays `mini:moved` / `dice:moved` to everyone else. Others ease toward each update.

**Verified live** (two browsers):
- A dragged mini lands at the same spot for both players.
- Another player can't move it.
- A dragged die moves for everyone, and clicking it rolls it.

The run caught the "scribble under someone else's mini" issue and the washed-out bases.

**Rejected:** flat coin tokens (the owner preferred minis). Also rejected: acked moves, since a round-trip per drag frame adds latency for nothing, and per-player locking (the host override covers game-master needs).

## 2026-09-25 — Synced clips: the YouTube clip is shared state with a playback anchor; anyone controls it unless the host locks it

**Decided (owner: "anyone" may control; the host can lock).** The clip everyone watches is no longer a per-browser player that starts when a `sound:play` broadcast arrives. It's `GameState.clip`: the video, whether it's playing, and a **playback anchor**, meaning the position at a moment on the server's clock. A YouTube soundboard entry starts it.
- **Controls:** `clip:control` (`play` / `pause` / `seek` / `stop` / `ended`) re-anchors it and patches everyone.
- **Lock:** `clip:lock` (host only) restricts control to the host. `ended` isn't blocked, since it's not anyone's choice.
- **Stale controls:** each play gets a fresh id, so a control for an older clip is refused.
- **What the clip state brings:**
  - Late joiners land at the right moment.
  - A saved table keeps its clip.
  - Your volume stays your own.
  - A clip that can't play in one browser is hidden there only.

**The client follows the anchor** (`YouTubeClip.tsx`, decisions pure in `clipSync.ts`):
- Target = anchor + elapsed time on the server's clock. The join ack now carries `serverNow`, giving the client its clock offset.
- It seeks when it's more than 1.5 s off, and plays or pauses to match.
- YouTube's own controls are read back from the player: state changes cover play and pause, and a seek shows up as a position jump between 1 s polls that elapsed time can't explain. Those are sent to everyone, or undone while the host has locked the TV.
- The TV banner and the corner card get play/pause for everyone, the lock, and stop for everyone.

**Two things live testing caught** (three browsers, a dev-only `__clip` hook to read and drive the players):
- A seek makes YouTube buffer, and resetting the poll's "before" reading during buffering hid the jump. Now the pre-buffer reading is kept, with only its clock moved.
- A seek right after applying someone else's change fell into a gap with no baseline. It looked like drift and was snapped back. Now the poll always tracks, and only *acting* waits while our own change settles.

After both fixes, players stayed within 0.1 s of each other and a late joiner within 0.4 s, over repeated runs.

**Rejected:** relaying raw player events peer to peer without server state (late joiners and saved tables couldn't know where the clip is). Also rejected: YouTube's own "watch party" features, which don't exist for embeds.

**Same day, the fire sound** "getting lost": it faded quadratically to silence by 8 m, which left it at 2–7% at the table and at nothing in the lifted table view. It now has a room-filling 30% floor, and distance is measured across the floor.

## 2026-09-25 — Saved tables: tables survive restarts, and an empty table waits 7 days for its host (same code, or a secret host link)

**Decided with the owner:**
- Save for **both** goals, surviving restarts and carrying a campaign between game nights.
- A table comes back by **hosting the same code**.
- Only the **host** can reopen it, from their own browser or anywhere with a **secret host link**.
- An empty table is kept for **7 days**.

The discussion options and the answers are in the changelog (2026-09-25, "Saved tables").

**How:**
- **What gets saved:** one JSON file per table (`server/src/tableArchive.ts`), holding the whole `GameState` plus the table's host key and, for players still seated, a SHA-256 of their secret token (never the token).
  - The file is written atomically (temp file + rename).
  - Deployed, the files live on a volume (`/srv/apps/tabletop/tables`); in dev they go in `server/data/tables` (gitignored), so a `tsx watch` restart keeps your table too. Tests run without persistence (no `tablesDir`), as before.
- **When it's saved:** every 5 s, each table in play is fingerprinted (SHA-256 of its JSON) and written only if it changed.
  - That catches every mutation path by construction. Hooking a "dirty" flag into ~30 socket handlers could silently miss one.
  - It also saves on shutdown (SIGTERM), and at the moment the last player leaves.
  - A hard kill loses at most 5 s. Verified by killing the server process mid-game (Windows can't deliver SIGTERM, so this ran without the graceful save): the table came back, and the page reconnected into it by itself.
- **After a restart:** tables that were in play come back with their players marked away, and each player gets the usual reconnect grace period. The existing auto-rejoin (`resume`) then just works: the same player id, with the token checked against its hash. A deploy mid-game is now a few-second blip.
- **Reopening:**
  - A code with no live table but a save on disk is refused (`TABLE_WAITING_FOR_HOST_ERROR`) unless the join brings the table's host key (constant-time comparison). With the key, the table is restored empty and the joiner becomes host ("… reopened the table").
  - A new host picking a saved table's code is refused the same way, and the join screen says the code is taken.
  - Guests waiting on the join screen re-check every 3 s, so the Join button lights up when the host reopens.
- **The host key:**
  - A random key per table. The join ack gives it only to the host. Whoever *becomes* host (a handover, or the host leaving) gets it privately (`session:host-key`); it's never broadcast.
  - The browser keeps host keys in **localStorage**, because the player identity is per tab (sessionStorage) and can't recognise "the host's browser" across visits. The host link (`?join=CODE&host=KEY`) carries the same key to any device; the app stores it and strips it from the address bar at once.
  - Unlike player tokens, the host key is stored in plain form in the table's file. It has to be shown again, to whichever player is host, for the link. It unlocks only that one table, and reading it needs the server's disk.
- **Housekeeping:**
  - Saved tables expire 7 days after their last use. There's a cap of 500 tables / 512 MB, evicting the least recently used saved (never live) tables.
  - Uploads a saved table uses are protected from upload pruning until the table goes.
  - Session codes are capped at 32 characters (the client makes 5 and allows 12). A code that isn't plain `[A-Z0-9_-]` gets a hashed file name.
  - Saves carry `format: 1`. An older save is filled in with today's defaults when restored; a newer one is skipped, not misread.

**Rejected:**
- SQLite: `better-sqlite3` is a native build in the Docker image, and `node:sqlite` is still marked experimental. A few hundred small JSON files don't need a database.
- Accounts, to own tables: far more than a friend group needs.
- Recognising the host by browser only: it's lost with the browser data. The host link is the owner's chosen remedy.
- A downloadable save file: uploads would bloat it, or go missing once pruned. It stays in the in-discussion list if ever wanted.

## 2026-09-25 — Deployment: one container at tabletop.murri.me behind the owner's Nginx Proxy Manager; open access with upload safeguards

**Decided.** The game runs on the owner's VPS as one Docker container. The Node server also serves the built client, so the page, uploads and Socket.IO share one origin and port. It's a new compose project in `/srv/apps/tabletop` on the box's shared `murrinet` network, reached by Nginx Proxy Manager as `tabletop:3001`, with nothing published on the host. Full setup: [engineering/deployment.md](engineering/deployment.md).

- **One container, not two** (a static host plus an API). The alternative, an nginx container for the client and a Node one for the API, would mean two things to deploy and a cross-origin setup. Served together, the client just uses its own origin, and uploads' absolute URLs stay valid.
- **The server is bundled with esbuild**, with `shared/` inlined and the four runtime packages external. The runtime image then needs only production dependencies: no `tsx`, no TypeScript, no dev tooling. That's also why the 5 `npm audit` findings (all Vite/Vitest dev tooling; `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 0) don't reach the deployed app.
- **Deploys ship the exact commit over SSH** (`scripts/deploy.mjs`: `git archive` → the VPS builds and restarts via `scripts/deploy-remote.sh`). The local side is Node, not bash: from PowerShell or cmd, `bash` is the WSL launcher (no distro installed here), so a bash `npm run deploy` failed with `execvpe(/bin/bash)`. There's no registry login and no dependency on GitHub, and no push is needed to deploy — pushing stays the separate, asked-for step the git workflow requires. Rejected: pushing images to Docker Hub or GHCR (credentials on both ends) and building locally (needs Docker Desktop running; ships ~150 MB per deploy).
- **The VPS runs critical services**, so the container is fenced in: 512 MB memory, 1 CPU, a PID limit, a read-only root filesystem, all capabilities dropped, no-new-privileges, and capped logs. Every VPS change is additive. `/srv/manage.sh`, NPM's config and the `/srv` git repo are untouched; `manage.sh` names its services explicitly, so its "all" never touches this one.
- **Open access with upload safeguards** (the owner's choice over a password-gated site or no limits). Anyone with the link can play. Uploads are pruned when no live session uses them and they're older than 24 h (sessions are in-memory, so those files are unreachable). They're also capped at 2 GB, evicting the oldest unused first. A file in use, or younger than 10 minutes, is never deleted. Past the cap, uploads get a 507, and each IP gets at most 30 uploads per 10 minutes (`TRUST_PROXY` lets Express see real IPs behind NPM).
- **The NPM proxy host is created by the owner** in NPM's UI. That needs their login, which Claude doesn't handle. Settings: websockets on, block exploits on, asset caching off (the server sets its own cache headers), Let's Encrypt, Force SSL.
- **Cache headers:** Vite's hashed `assets/` are immutable for a year. `index.html`, the room model and the characters are `no-cache` (revalidated, a cheap 304), so a redeploy shows up on the next reload.
- **The e2e smoke tests can target any URL** (`E2E_BASE_URL`). They measure the rendered room from a real screenshot rather than a dev-only debug hook, so they run against a production build and against the live site. All 9 runs passed against the local production build before deploying.

## 2026-09-25 — The polish pass: themed in-game controls, a parchment table, a self-checking TV, silent-without-Web-Audio, lights off the walls

A full visual and functional QA pass over every feature, with scripted Playwright tours on the real GPU: screenshots of every view, dialog and menu, in all three engines. Decisions it produced:

1. **One theme for in-game controls, at zero specificity.** The session menu, dialogs and wall-board menu used browser-default buttons, fields, file pickers and dropdowns next to a polished join card. One block now styles them all in the join screen's palette:
   - Everything is wrapped in `:where()`, so every component's own button style (tabs, die picker, slot grid, grid picker) still wins without a specificity war.
   - The variables live on `:root`, not `.game-shell`: the whiteboard and map-crop dialogs are portaled to `<body>`, and on `.game-shell` they silently got no gold at all.
   - The fire-sound checkbox became a switch; settings are label-and-control rows.
2. **The session menu collapses to its header**, remembered per browser as a client setting (`menuCollapsed`), so the room can be seen whole. The invite button became icon + "Invite" so the header fits on one line.
3. **The bare table is aged parchment**, painted procedurally once per page (`parchment.ts`): mottling, fibres, cup rings, worn edges, a compass rose. It's **seeded**, so every player sees the same sheet. A texture asset would cost a download on the join path; this costs ~10 ms. The map-crop bake paints the same sheet behind a map's margins.
4. **The TV's CSS layer runs in millimetres and checks itself.** In metres the 800px player was scaled by ~0.001, and WebKit measured the screen at 1x1px; the layer (scene and a mirrored camera) is now scaled by 1000, with identical placement in Chromium and Firefox. Playwright's Windows WebKit still misplaces 3D-transformed layers.
   - **Rejected:** sniffing the user agent for Safari. It's the wrong question: macOS Safari may well be fine, and this WebKit build may not be representative.
   - **Chosen:** the TV compares where its player lands with where the screen projects, on its first frames in view. After 10 misplaced frames it retires, and clips play in the corner player, resuming where they were.
5. **No Web Audio means silence, not a crash.** `getAudioContext()` falls back to `webkitAudioContext` and returns null when neither exists; every caller treats null as "don't play". Playwright's Windows WebKit build has no AudioContext at all, and the first sound threw a page error.
6. **Clips resume when moved** between the TV and the corner card: a module-level resume point (key + seconds) is saved when a player unmounts. Portaling into a new host remounts the iframe, which restarted the video.
7. **Lights sit off the surfaces they light.** Three hotspots traced back to one cause: a point light within centimetres of a surface, where inverse-square falloff blows out. The wall-candle cluster lights sat on the wall between the sconces; they're now pulled ~0.55 m toward the room. The chandelier's bounce light hung just under the lantern and turned its ironwork into white patches; it now hangs 0.6 m lower. Rule of thumb for this room: keep `PointLight`s ≥ 0.5 m from any mesh they aren't meant to glow on.
8. **Blender fixes:**
   - The kite shield's painted body was offset from its metal trim, reading as a pale smear from the table. It's now re-seated and centered on the chimney.
   - The firebox floor was pale slate lit near-white; it's soot now.
   - The ember bed gets a runtime glowing-coal emissive map instead of one flat emissive sheet.
9. **The wall board highlights the button you're aiming at** (an emissive glow in its own color, standing 12 mm proud), so it's clear what E will press. That completes the live check deferred on 2026-09-24: aim + E plays, an empty button opens the assign menu, and Shift+E reassigns.

Measured after the pass: 60 fps (vsync-bound) at 1080p with 135 draw calls and ~160k triangles, on the real GPU. `THREE.Clock` (deprecated in r186) became `THREE.Timer`, and the console is clean.

## 2026-09-25 — Cross-browser smoke tests with Playwright (Chromium, Firefox, WebKit); headless Chromium on the real GPU

**Decided.** `npm run test:e2e` runs `e2e/smoke.spec.ts` against the real app in all three engines: host a table and check that the room renders a non-black frame with no console errors, and a second player joins, both see each other, chat reaches the other side and a typed `/roll` lands in both logs. `playwright.config.ts` starts the dev server and client itself, or reuses ones already running. This closes Milestone 9's missing browser pass — Firefox and WebKit (Safari's engine) could never be driven here before.

**Why Playwright.** It ships its own Firefox and WebKit builds, so a Windows machine can test Safari's engine without a Mac. The alternatives were Cypress (no WebKit) and Selenium/WebDriver (needs each real browser installed, no WebKit on Windows).

**Gotcha: headless Chromium defaults to SwiftShader** (software WebGL). It rendered the room so slowly that the two-player test ran out of time. On Windows the config passes `--use-angle=d3d11` to use the real GPU (~3.6 s instead of ~90 s). Firefox and WebKit already use the GPU headless.

## 2026-09-24 — Identity binding, presence with a reconnect grace period, host handover, resume-only rejoin

**Decided**, as groundwork for the second change-request batch (player colors/models, whiteboard, 3-player sync). A code-reading pass found that Milestone 9's "host authority" was only half true: the server trusted the `playerId` inside every payload, and every client can read the host's id straight out of `GameState.hostId`, so any player could perform any host action by pasting the host's id into a forged event. M9's tests only proved "a non-host using *their own* id is rejected."

1. **A socket's identity is fixed by its own successful `session:join` and remembered server-side** (`server.ts`'s `identities` map). Every later event is checked against that binding (`actsAs` / `playerIn`); a mismatched payload is rejected (`'Not joined to this session as that player.'`) or, for fire-and-forget events, dropped. Payload shapes were left unchanged (they still carry `playerId`) — the server just stops *trusting* it — to avoid churning every event type. **Rules out:** any handler reading authority from a payload field.
2. **Rejoining as an existing player requires a per-tab secret `playerToken`**, generated next to `playerId` in `sessionStorage` and held by `SessionStore` outside `GameState` (never broadcast). Without it, binding alone would be pointless — an attacker would just `session:join` *as* the host's public id. A same-identity join from a newer socket takes over and the old socket gets `session:replaced` (typically a duplicated browser tab, which copies `sessionStorage`).
3. **Disconnect ≠ leave is refined, not reversed (M2 decision): a dropped player is marked `connected: false` for a 45 s grace period, then removed exactly as if they'd left.** M2's "a dropped player stays in the roster forever" was acceptable with capsules and no presence UI, but with real character models a closed tab would leave a frozen figure in the room indefinitely. The grace period keeps reloads/blips seamless (they reappear instantly; everyone else sees them ghosted and "reconnecting…" in the meantime). Grace is a `createAppServer({ disconnectGraceMs })` option so tests can shorten it.
4. **Host migration (deferred since M2) is done**: when the host leaves or times out, the host role passes to the longest-present connected player. The host can also hand it over explicitly (`session:transfer-host`, host-gated, a "Make host" button in the Players tab).
5. **Drawing strokes can only be extended by their author** (`appendDrawingPoint` checks the bound actor). Erasing any stroke stays open to everyone, as designed in M8.
6. **Automatic rejoins use `resume: true`, which never creates a session.** A reload now drops the player straight back into the room (the last join is remembered per tab in `sessionStorage`, `client/src/joinMemory.ts`), and a socket reconnect re-sends the join as before — but if the session is gone (e.g. a server restart wiped the in-memory state), the join fails with `SESSION_ENDED_ERROR` and the player sees "Session X has ended" instead of silently becoming host of a brand-new empty room. An in-game "Connection lost — reconnecting…" banner covers the gap.
7. **Keyboard hygiene**: the interact key ignores auto-repeat and anything typed into a text field (`client/src/keyboard.ts`, unit-tested) — previously, typing a URL containing "e" into the session menu while seated stood the player up; movement keys likewise ignore text fields and are released on window blur / pointer-lock loss (no more walking on by themselves after alt-tab); the keybind-rebind capture runs in the capture phase so the pressed key can't also trigger the room.

**Verified:** `server/src/identity.test.ts` (10 tests, new): forged-host-id rejected with no effect (break-round done — with the check removed, the forged `scene:update` succeeds and the test fails), identity takeover without the token rejected, an un-joined socket can't mutate, another player's stroke can't be extended, reconnect takeover + `session:replaced`, grace-period removal, in-time reconnect survives the timer, host handover on timeout, `session:transfer-host` host-gated, resume-join on a gone session fails cleanly. `sessionStore.test.ts` extended (credentials, presence, removal, host migration, transfer). Client: `keyboard.test.ts`, `joinMemory.test.ts`, `playerIdentity.test.ts`, `PlayerAvatars.test.ts` (ghosting). Live two-tab check (Chrome, in-app browser): both players see each other; a reload shows "(reconnecting…)" to the other player and the reloaded tab lands straight back in the room as the same player; stopping the server shows the banner, restarting it shows "Session SMOKE has ended". Full sanity (lint/format/build/test — 282 tests) clean.

## 2026-09-24 — Session menu rework: categorized tabs, client-only settings, rebindable interact key, direct-link sounds — and a hard no to a YouTube audio downloader

**Decided**, a post-M9/pre-M10 user-requested extension (not itself a roadmap milestone — a UI/UX pass on the existing session menu and soundboard, prompted directly by the user rather than the roadmap's own sequencing). Bundled five related asks from one message: restructure the top-right session menu into categorized tabs; center text and use consistent spacing/icons; add client-only settings (starting with volume, designed to grow); make the interact key rebindable; and let any player add sounds to the soundboard via a link, "for example... a youtube link."

1. **Refused to build any YouTube audio extraction/download mechanism, regardless of implementation approach** — flagged this to the user directly rather than quietly picking a workaround or quietly declining. Downloading/re-hosting audio ripped from YouTube violates YouTube's ToS and is generally copyright infringement of the uploaded content, independent of *how* it's technically implemented (server-side `youtube-dl`-style extraction, client-side scraping, etc.) — this is a hard constraint, not a style preference, so it wasn't something to design around silently. Offered three legitimate alternatives via `AskUserQuestion`; the user picked **direct links to already-hosted audio files** (mp3/wav/ogg — the same mechanism as an uploaded file, just referenced by URL instead of uploaded as bytes). **Rules out:** any feature that fetches, transcodes, or proxies audio from a video-hosting site on the user's behalf.
2. **Settings are client-only `localStorage` state, deliberately never synced to the server or `GameState`** — volume and the interact keybind are per-browser preferences, not shared session state; a player rebinding "interact" to `F` doesn't affect what key anyone else uses. Followed the exact dependency-injection pattern `playerIdentity.ts` already established for storage-backed client code (`SettingsStorage` interface taking `getItem`/`setItem`, storage passed as a parameter rather than read from the `localStorage` global directly), specifically so `settings.ts` stays unit-testable under Vitest's `node` test environment (no jsdom, so `window.localStorage` isn't available in tests). **Rules out:** any settings value needing to become shared state later without a deliberate migration — the type is `ClientSettings`, intentionally distinct from `GameState`, and nothing currently bridges the two.
3. **React Context split across three files** (`settingsContextValue.ts`: the `createContext` call + type; `SettingsContext.tsx`: only the `SettingsProvider` component; `useSettings.ts`: only the consuming hook) — driven by `eslint-plugin-react-refresh`'s `only-export-components` rule, which wants a file to export either components only or non-component values only, not a mix. This is now the established pattern for any future Context in this codebase, not a one-off.
4. **The interact key is stored as a raw `KeyboardEvent.code` string** (e.g. `"KeyE"`, `"KeyF"`), matching the convention `FirstPersonController.ts`'s WASD handling already uses (`.code`, not `.key`, for layout independence), with a small `formatKeyCode()` helper (`client/src/keyLabel.ts`) converting it to a human-readable label only at display time. The rebind UI refuses to bind onto any of the four movement keys (`FirstPersonController.ts` now exports `MOVEMENT_KEYS` for this exact check) rather than silently letting a player break their own WASD — caught and shown as an inline error, rebind mode stays open so they can try again; `Escape` cancels without changing anything.
5. **A sound added via URL gets its display name auto-derived from the URL's last path segment** (`client/src/soundName.ts`'s `deriveNameFromUrl` — strips the extension, decodes URI-encoding, swaps `-`/`_` for spaces, falls back to `"Sound"` if nothing usable is found) rather than prompting for a separate name field — kept the "add a sound" form to one input, matching the file-upload path's existing behavior of deriving a name from the filename.
6. **Volume is applied across both of this app's audio-source types via two different mechanisms**, unified under one 0–1 `masterVolume` in `sounds.ts`: `GainNode.gain` peak-value scaling for the built-in synthesized tones, and the `HTMLAudioElement.volume` property for uploaded/linked file playback. No single Web Audio API covers both, since the built-in sounds were never routed through an `<audio>` element (see the Milestone 7 decision that they're synthesized tones, not shipped files).

## 2026-09-24 — Milestone 8: room interactables — one generic `object:interact` event, proximity + E over raycast/click, the table's sit-down mode reuses existing camera/avatar machinery, a physical soundboard console reuses `sound:play` as-is

**Decided**, closing out Milestone 8. Scope was expanded live with the user beyond the roadmap's original one-line description ("additional 3D objects... a dice tray, shelf, etc.") — asked what M8's interactable(s) should actually be and how they should trigger, and the user specified three: the table switching between "wander the room" and "sit at the table" modes, a toggleable light, and (explicitly offered as optional, "maybe... cook something cool and funny up for this") a physical wall/floor soundboard console with large clickable buttons. All three landed.

1. **One generic `object:interact` event (`shared/src/interactables.ts`) covers every interactable, dispatched server-side by a string `objectId`** (`server/src/sessionStore.ts`'s `toggleLight`/`toggleSeated`, chosen in a `switch` in `server.ts`), rather than a dedicated event per object — this is the event name `docs/engineering/architecture.md` already suggested ("will need their own event(s), e.g. `object:interact`") written literally. Neither mutation is host-gated: `toggleLight` flips a session-wide flag (nothing to protect — it affects everyone equally, like a real light switch), and `toggleSeated` only ever flips the *requesting* player's own `seated` flag (the payload's `playerId` is always the actor, never a target), so there's no way to sit another player down. **Rules out:** a per-object event/type explosion for what's currently two behaviors, and host-gating either — reserved for if a future interactable actually needs it.
2. **Trigger model is proximity + a keypress (E), not raycast/click like table drawing** — an explicit user choice over the alternative (asked via `AskUserQuestion`). A pure `nearestInteractable(position, interactables)` function (`client/src/three/interaction.ts`, unit-tested) checks XZ distance each frame against a small fixed list of `{id, position, range}` entries; a `document`-level `keydown` listener for `KeyE` triggers whichever is nearest. Deliberately *not* gated by pointer-lock state (unlike drawing, which contends with the mouse for both look and draw) — a player can trigger an interactable whether they're mid-WASD-walk (pointer locked) or not, since E doesn't compete with anything. **Exception, logged as a deliberate deviation from the blanket "E" answer:** the soundboard console's individual buttons are raycast/click-triggered instead, gated the same way table drawing is (mouse free, not pointer-locked) — a single keypress can't disambiguate *which* of several buttons, so proximity+click (the established precedent from Milestone 5) fits better there than proximity+E does. Surfaced and accepted as a sensible carve-out, not silently done.
3. **The table's "sit down" mode is implemented as a pure camera takeover (`FirstPersonController.sit()`/`stand()`), not a new player state machine.** Sitting stores the current camera position/quaternion, releases pointer lock (so the *already-existing* raycast-driven interactions — drawing, dice clicks, the soundboard console — work immediately without any new code, since they're already gated on "not locked"), and repositions the camera to hover above the table looking straight down; `update()`'s existing `if (!isLocked) return` early-exit disables WASD for free, with no separate "is seated" movement guard needed. Standing restores the exact prior position/orientation and leaves the player to resume via the ordinary "click to look around" overlay (deliberately **not** auto-relocking pointer lock — some browsers only grant `requestPointerLock()` from a direct click gesture, and a silent relock would be inconsistent with every other unlocked state in this app). **Rules out:** teleporting the player's actual `position` to a seat slot (would need new collision/seat-assignment logic for no real benefit, since avatars are still placeholder capsules) — only the *visual* squash communicates "seated" to everyone else (see next point), never a position change.
4. **A seated player's `Player.seated: boolean` is genuine shared `GameState`, broadcast like any other ack'd mutation — but the local camera's sit/stand is applied optimistically on the keypress, not gated on the round-trip.** Two independent effects triggered by one E-press: `controller.sit()`/`stand()` fires immediately (a pure client-side view decision, no reason to wait on a network round-trip for a local camera cut), while `onObjectInteract('table')` separately updates server state so *other* players' view of this player updates too (`PlayerAvatars` squashes/lowers a seated player's capsule to ~55% height — the clearest "visible to everyone" signal available without real character animations). A rejoin/reload while `GameState` already has the player seated (the flag lingers across a disconnect, same as every other per-player status in this project) is reconciled once at room-load time by checking the fresh `GameState.players` snapshot and calling `controller.sit()` immediately if needed — without this, a reloaded client's local `isSeated` would start `false` while the server still says `true`, and the next E-press would desync the two by toggling the server *back* to standing instead of sitting the rejoined player down.
5. **`player:move` is skipped entirely while seated** (`RoomView.tsx`'s animate loop no-ops the whole send-if-moved block when `controller.isSeated`) — necessary, not just tidy: without this, the camera's teleport to/from the top-down hover position would itself register as a large `player:move` delta and broadcast the player's position as floating above the table to everyone else, which is wrong (the server-side `position` field is deliberately never touched by sit/stand, only the client-local camera is).
6. **The soundboard console (`client/src/three/SoundboardConsole.ts`) is presentation only — it adds zero new server logic**, reusing `sound:play`'s existing host-gate/ack/broadcast exactly as the 2D panel does. Its buttons are rebuilt from `GameState.soundboard` on every change (same `sync()`-from-full-snapshot pattern `DiceManager` uses), capped at 6 — the rest of a growing (built-in + uploaded) soundboard stays reachable through the existing 2D panel. Button size was deliberately generous (`BUTTON_RADIUS = 0.16`, doubled from an initial smaller pass) after live testing showed a small button is a genuinely difficult click target at typical in-room viewing distance — "large buttons" per the user's own framing turned out to be a real usability point, not just flavor text.
7. **A real, reproducible bug was caught and fixed during live two-tab verification, not by code review:** the E-press prompt text ("sit at the table" vs. "stand up") failed to update on the sit/stand transition, because the change-detection check that gates `setInteractionPrompt` compared only the *nearest interactable id* — which stays `'table'` in both the "standing near it" and "seated at it" states — never noticing that the `seated` flag (which `promptFor()` also depends on) had changed. Fixed by deduping on a combined `${nearestId}|${seated}` key (`lastPromptKeyRef`) instead of `nearestId` alone. Caught by directly driving the camera position via an injected debug hook and a real `KeyE` keypress (Chrome blocks synthetic pointer-lock clicks needed to drive WASD under automation, the same limitation logged since Milestone 3 — this is the first milestone where that gap was worked around well enough to catch a real bug live rather than resting entirely on unit tests).
8. **Sitting also switches the viewport to a square frame** (`RoomView.tsx`'s `applyViewportSize`, called from `FirstPersonController`'s camera + the mount effect's `renderer`/`container`), a follow-up user request ("the table should be full screen... it therefore needs to be square") once they saw the seated view in action. A round table naturally reads as "full screen" in a square frame, not a widescreen one — a widescreen aspect at a fixed vertical FOV either crops the table's top/bottom or leaves the table looking small with wasted space on the sides. `SEATED_HEIGHT_ABOVE_TABLE` (`FirstPersonController.ts`) was retuned from 2.1m to 1.65m to match — computed from the fixed 70° vertical FOV so the table's own radius fills the now-equal-on-both-axes frame with a small margin. The square canvas is centered via `.room-view`'s flexbox (harmless when it's full-size too, since standing's square-vs-full distinction collapses to "same size" there). **Rules out:** a dedicated orthographic top-down camera or a separate 2D-canvas overlay for the seated view — reusing the same perspective camera with a resized viewport is a two-function change, not a second rendering path.
9. **A small drawing toolbar (pen color/size, plus an eraser) appears only while seated** — the other half of the same follow-up request. `Drawing` gained `color`/`width` fields (`shared/src/types.ts`), chosen once when a stroke starts and carried on `DrawingStartRequest` (validated server-side with a `[1, 40]` width bound, `validation.ts`) — not resent per point, same "only the delta" principle the event already followed. `TableCanvas` now paints each stroke in its own stored style instead of one hardcoded color/width for the whole table. The eraser reuses the *existing*, previously-unwired `drawing:delete` event (implemented and unit-tested since Milestone 5, never actually triggered by any UI until now) via a new pure hit-test, `client/src/three/eraser.ts`'s `findStrokeNear` — point-to-segment distance to each stroke's polyline, minus half its own line width so a thick stroke is as easy to hit as it looks. **Rules out:** a per-point color/width (unnecessary — nothing in this app lets mid-stroke style changes happen) and a dedicated "delete a stroke" event (the exact semantics `drawing:delete` already had).
10. **Wiring up `drawing:delete` surfaced a genuine, previously-latent bug in its Milestone 5 implementation**, caught while implementing the eraser: `RoomView.tsx`'s `handleDrawingDelete` (the listener for *other* players' deletions) redrew from `activeSceneRef.current`, which still contained the just-deleted stroke — `drawing:delete` is a fire-and-forget delta event with no accompanying `session:state` broadcast, so a remote client's own copy of the scene never actually got the removal applied to it before the redraw ran, silently re-painting the stroke it was supposed to erase. The M5 exit-check test never caught this because it only asserted the broadcast *payload* arrived correctly, not that a receiving client's own derived visual state reflected it. Fixed by filtering the deleted drawing out of `activeSceneRef.current` locally before redrawing (mirroring the same local-optimistic-update pattern the eraser's own sender-side path needs for the identical reason) — a later unrelated `session:state` naturally overwrites this with the server's authoritative copy. Verified live with two tabs: erasing a stroke on one tab correctly removed it from the other's rendered table, not just from the eraser's own view.
11. **A same-session automation-testing gotcha worth recording**: verifying the color picker by setting `<input type="color">`'s `.value` directly and dispatching a plain `Event('input')` silently fails to trigger React's `onChange` — React's controlled-input tracking compares against its own internally tracked previous value and doesn't detect a value set outside its own setter, so the DOM swatch visually updates (native browser behavior) while the app's state never changes. The color stroke that "didn't work" during a first testing pass was this, not an app bug — confirmed by re-testing with the native value setter (`Object.getOwnPropertyDescriptor(HTMLInputElement.prototype, 'value').set`) before dispatching, and by inspecting the table's raw 2D canvas pixel data directly (bypassing the 3D scene's warm lighting/tone-mapping, which independently desaturates a chosen color quite a bit when rendered on the lit table surface — the same visual-only distortion already logged for the map-image upload). Two lessons for next time this pattern comes up: use the native setter for any React-controlled-input automation test, and when verifying a canvas-texture color end-to-end, check the underlying canvas's own pixel data rather than eyeballing the lit 3D render.
12. **The eraser's actual first user report ("worked once, then broken") turned out to be a real, more serious bug than point 10's** — not a click-precision issue as first suspected, and not something the earlier two-tab verification pass happened to catch, because that pass's incidental `sound:play`/reconciliation broadcasts (which fully refresh `activeSceneRef.current` from the server) papered over it by accident. The actual defect: `activeSceneRef.current.drawings` was never updated in real time as strokes were drawn — neither the local player's own `onStrokeStart`/`onStrokePoint` handlers nor the remote `handleDrawingStart`/`handleDrawingUpdate` listeners touched it, only the visible canvas texture did. Since `drawing:*` has no accompanying `session:state` broadcast (by design, per `docs/engineering/architecture.md`'s "Performance" section), a freshly-drawn stroke — by any player — was invisible to `findStrokeNear`'s hit-test until some *unrelated* event happened to trigger a full state refresh. In practice this meant erasing usually failed for anything drawn in the current sitting, working only for strokes that predated it (e.g. from before a reload) or when the player happened to also click a sound/light first. Fixed by updating `activeSceneRef.current.drawings` directly in all four handlers (append on start, append-point on update, mirroring the same pattern already used for the delete/erase paths) — verified by drawing a brand-new stroke and immediately erasing it, locally and (separately) across two tabs, with no incidental broadcast in between either time. **Rules out:** treating `drawing:*`'s delta-only design as "fire and forget locally too" — anything that needs to *read back* the current stroke list (not just paint it) has to maintain its own copy in step with every delta, the same requirement `drawing:delete`'s fix (point 10) already established for deletions specifically.

**Verified:** `server/src/interactables.test.ts` (4 tests) — a fresh session starts with the light on and nobody seated; one player's light toggle is seen live by another and can be toggled back off/on, not host-gated; a player sitting only ever changes their *own* seated status, seen live by everyone, and can stand back up; an unrecognized `objectId` and a malformed payload are both rejected without crashing. Break-round done on the `session:state` broadcast (disabled, confirmed both "seen live" assertions correctly fail/time out, restored). `client/src/three/interaction.test.ts` (5 tests) and `client/src/three/eraser.test.ts` (6 tests) cover the proximity and stroke-hit-test math in isolation. `client/src/three/PlayerAvatars.test.ts` extended (now 8 tests) — a seated player's avatar squashes, and `updateOne` (driven by `player:move`, which a seated player never sends) is confirmed to always apply as standing, documenting why the RoomView-level gating in point 5 above is load-bearing. `server/src/validation.test.ts`/`sessionStore.test.ts`/`tabletopMap.test.ts` extended for `Drawing`'s new `color`/`width` fields, including a width-out-of-bounds rejection case. ## 2026-09-24 — Milestone 7: soundboard & mute — synthesized tones instead of audio assets, self-mute plus host moderation, sender included in the sound broadcast

**Decided**, closing out Milestone 7.

1. **`sound:play` is host-only** (the roadmap's own stated default scope, taken as-is rather than re-litigated), while **`player:mute`/`player:unmute` are not purely host-gated: a player can always mute/unmute *themselves* (self-service, like muting your own mic), and the host can additionally mute/unmute *any* player (moderation)** — `SessionStore.setMuted` checks `actorId === targetPlayerId || isHost` rather than requiring host for every call. `docs/engineering/architecture.md`'s authority list names "Spieler muten" as needing server validation without saying who may trigger it; self-service mute is such an expected baseline capability (nobody should need to ask the host to mute their own mic) that leaving it out felt like an oversight in the spec, not a deliberate restriction. **Rules out:** requiring the host for every mute/unmute call, which would make a player unable to mute themselves.
2. **`muted` is a visible status flag only — this app has no voice chat to actually silence.** Worth stating plainly since the original spec's field name invites the assumption that muting does something functional; there's no microphone/audio-stream feature anywhere in this project to mute. The M7 exit check ("a muted player's state is visible to the group") is exactly this: a status indicator, not audio control.
3. **Soundboard sounds are synthesized tones (Web Audio `OscillatorNode`), not audio asset files.** No real sound effect files are available in this project, and generating one from a `{frequency, waveform, durationMs}` triple needs no asset pipeline at all — the same placeholder-quality tradeoff as colored capsule avatars instead of real character models, or a parchment canvas instead of real map art. A single shared, lazily-created `AudioContext` (not one per play) is reused across plays and `.resume()`d if suspended, handling the browser autoplay policy uniformly.
4. **`sound:play`'s server broadcast includes the sender** (`io.to(sessionId)`, not `socket.to(sessionId)` like `player:move`/`drawing:*`), and the client never plays a sound optimistically on trigger — every client, host included, only plays once the broadcast arrives. Unlike drawing/movement, there's no local-prediction reason to special-case the sender: a sound has no persisted GameState to update, so the broadcast *is* the only place playback is triggered from, and skipping the sender would mean the host who clicked the button never hears their own cue. **Rules out:** reusing `player:move`/`drawing:*`'s sender-excluded delta pattern here — it doesn't fit an event with no local optimistic effect to avoid double-applying.

## 2026-09-23 — Milestone 4: player avatars — yaw-only rotation, delta-only `player:move`, membership vs. live-movement split on the client

**Decided**, four related calls implementing Milestone 4.

1. **`Player` gained `rotationY` (yaw only, radians) — no pitch/roll.** A placeholder capsule avatar only needs to face a direction; tracking full camera orientation would be unused data pushed over the wire on every move. Computed from `camera.getWorldDirection()` (`FirstPersonController.getYaw()`), not read off `camera.rotation.y` directly — `PointerLockControls` manages pitch/yaw internally via a `'YXZ'`-order Euler that doesn't match `Object3D.rotation`'s own default `'XYZ'` decomposition, so reading `.rotation.y` directly would be quietly wrong once pitch is non-zero. **Rules out:** syncing full camera quaternion/orientation — more data than a placeholder capsule can even display.
2. **`player:move` is fire-and-forget, not acked, and rebroadcast as the same small delta (`socket.to(sessionId).emit`, excluding the sender) rather than folded into a `session:state` full-state broadcast.** Matches `docs/engineering/architecture.md`'s "Performance" section directly ("nicht bei jeder Änderung der komplette Game State übertragen") — this event fires up to ~10 times/second per moving player, so a full-`GameState` broadcast or an ack round-trip on every one would scale badly with player count. The server still validates and applies it (`SessionStore.move`, shape-checked in `validation.ts`) so a later joiner's initial `session:state` reflects everyone's current position — a malformed payload is silently dropped rather than acked with an error, since there's no ack channel to put an error on in the first place. **Rules out:** an ack-based request/response for movement, and reusing `session:state` for per-frame position updates.
3. **Client-side, avatar *membership* (who has a capsule at all) and *live movement* (where it currently is) are deliberately different code paths, not one.** `PlayerAvatars.sync(players, selfId)` runs only from `GameState.players` — infrequent, only on join/leave. `PlayerAvatars.updateOne(...)` runs from live `player:move` broadcasts — frequent. If a single path handled both, either movement updates would get needlessly reconciled against a stale `GameState.players` array on every frame, or a join/leave would require re-deriving live positions it doesn't have. Kept as two methods on the same class instead of two classes, since they share the mesh map. **Rules out:** re-running `sync()` on every animation frame.
4. **A new player's server-side default position/rotation is a real spawn point (`DEFAULT_SPAWN_POSITION`, shared between client and server), not the origin.** First tried `{x:0,y:0,z:0}` — harmless in Milestone 2 (nothing rendered a token there), but once avatars render from it, a freshly-joined player's own placeholder position defaults to underground-at-the-origin (`y - PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` math with `y=0` goes negative) until their first `player:move` corrects it. Moved the constant into `shared/src/types.ts` so `RoomLayout.ts`'s `PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` and `sessionStore.ts`'s `createPlayer` default derive from the same value rather than two literals that could drift. **Rules out:** leaving the mismatch as a "flashes briefly, self-corrects" cosmetic bug — cheap enough to fix at the root once noticed.

**Verified:** `server/src/playerMove.test.ts` — two real `socket.io-client`s, one moves, the other receives the broadcast live and the mover does *not* receive their own echo; a later joiner's `session:state` reflects an already-moved player's position; a malformed move doesn't crash the server. ## 2026-09-23 — Milestone 3 part 1 implementation: axis-separated collision, `PointerLockControls`, room/table sizing

## 2026-09-23 — Milestone 2 design calls: implicit session creation, derived host role, disconnect ≠ leave, sessionStorage identity

**Decided**, four related calls made implementing Milestone 2 (`docs/roadmap.md`), none pinned down by the original spec:

1. **A session is created implicitly by its first joiner**, who becomes host. There's no `session:create` event — the spec's event list only has `session:join`/`session:leave`, and reading "join a code that doesn't exist yet" as "create it" avoids inventing an event the spec never named. **Rules out:** a separate creation flow/event for Milestone 2.
2. **Host/player role is derived, not stored per-player.** `GameState.hostId` already existed in the shared type; a player is host iff `player.id === state.hostId`. No new field on `Player`, no client-sent role claim possible — a client cannot fabricate host status because there's nothing to fabricate, only a comparison the server owns. **Host migration is explicitly out of scope**: if the host's player leaves, `hostId` keeps pointing at their now-absent id. Nothing crashes (no code dereferences `hostId` assuming a matching player exists), but there is currently no way for a session to get a new host once that happens. **Rules out:** building migration logic now — flagged for whichever milestone first needs host-gated actions to keep working after a host leaves (M5 `scene:*` is the first one that will need this).
3. **A raw socket disconnect does not remove the player from the session roster — only an explicit `session:leave` does.** This is what "a dropped client can rejoin the same session" (the M2 exit check) actually means: the `Player` record simply sits in `GameState.players` until either the same identity reconnects (`session:join` with the same `playerId` rebinds it to the new socket, no duplicate) or someone explicitly leaves. **Accepted downside:** a permanently-dropped client (closed tab, crashed browser) lingers in the roster forever, with no presence/timeout mechanism. **Rules out:** treating `disconnect` as an implicit leave, which would break reconnect entirely (the whole point of a dropped client rejoining is that their spot is still there).
4. **Client identity (`playerId`) is generated once and persisted in `sessionStorage`, not `localStorage`.** `localStorage` is shared across every tab of the same origin — two tabs of the same browser would collide onto one `playerId`, silently merging what should be two distinct players and breaking the "two browser tabs... see each other" exit check outright. `sessionStorage` is per-tab but still survives a same-tab reload, which is exactly the reconnect case this needs to support. **Rules out:** `localStorage` for this purpose, despite it being the more common default for "remember this across visits" — the per-tab requirement here overrides that.

**Verified:** `server/src/session.test.ts` connects two real `socket.io-client` instances against a real server and asserts each sees the other in the resulting player list, plus a disconnect/rejoin-same-identity/leave sequence. **Decided.** `server/tsconfig.json` and `client/tsconfig.json` originally declared a TypeScript project reference (`"references": [{ "path": "../shared" }]`) to `shared`. `tsc` rejected it: a referenced project must be `"composite": true` and may not disable emit, which conflicts with `shared` being consumed as plain TS source (`main`/`types` pointing straight at `src/index.ts`, no build step — see [engineering/tooling.md](engineering/tooling.md)). Dropped the `references` array from both; `@custom-tabletop/shared` still resolves correctly for both type-checking and runtime through ordinary Node module resolution via the npm-workspace symlink in `node_modules`, which reads `shared/package.json`'s `main`/`types` fields directly — no project-reference machinery needed for a package with no build step of its own. **Rules out:** using `tsc -b` for this repo unless `shared` later gains a real build step (e.g. if it needs to ship compiled output to something that isn't `tsx`/Vite). **Verified:** `npm run build` type-checks clean in all three workspaces.

## 2026-09-23 — Server port 3001, client dev port 5173 (Vite default); ping/pong added as an infra-only event pair

**Decided.** Server listens on `3001` (`PORT` env var, default), Vite serves the client on its default `5173`; the client reads the server URL from `VITE_SERVER_URL` (see `client/.env.example`). **Decided.** `npm install` surfaces 5 advisories (3 moderate, 1 high, 1 critical) in `vite`/`esbuild`/`vitest`'s dev-server request-handling code — all about a *local dev server* accepting requests it shouldn't, not a production runtime risk for a project with no Vite-dev-server-based deployment. ## 2026-09-23 — Player experience is a walkable 3D room, not a 2D map with 3D accents

**Decided.** The source spec ([engineering/architecture.md](engineering/architecture.md)) describes a 2D-canvas-first VTT — a top-down map filling the screen, with a Three.js layer floating 3D dice/figures over it (Roll20/Foundry model). The actual product vision is a **Blender-built 3D room** (a big table, walls, interactable furniture) that the player **walks around in free first-person** (WASD + mouse-look, collision). The map/drawing surface becomes a `THREE.CanvasTexture` applied to the table inside that room, rather than a full-screen 2D canvas.

**Why this still fits the spec rather than replacing it:** the drawing event model, delta-only sync, `GameState` shape (`Player.position` was already `x/y/z`, not 2D), and server-authority rules are untouched — only *where* things render changes. The spec itself scopes Three.js for "future 3D tabletop objects," which a room and its furniture are simply more of.

**What's genuinely new, not implied by the original spec:** a Blender → glTF/GLB → `GLTFLoader` asset pipeline, first-person movement/collision code, and room interactables (their own event, e.g. `object:interact`, server-validated like everything else).

**Rules out:** the flat top-down map as the primary/only view, and an orbit-only camera (considered and rejected in favor of full walk-around — more scope, but it's the actual vision, not a simplification of it). **Reorders the roadmap:** room + first-person movement now land *before* the tabletop map/drawing milestone, since the room is the primary view the map lives inside of, not an overlay on top of it — see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).

## 2026-09-23 — Repo workflow reused from `extraction_project`, trimmed to this project's scope

**Decided.** The docs/process structure (`CLAUDE.md` entry point, `docs/overview.md` + `decisions.md` + `changelog.md` as always-read logs, `docs/process/` for git workflow and session handover, `.claude/skills/` for `feature-workflow` and `sanity-check`) is carried over from the sibling `extraction_project` repo, which has iterated this pattern over many sessions. **Dropped:** the `parallel-agents` skill/doc and its worktree-based subagent orchestration — that repo is a large multi-system Unreal project where independent features genuinely parallelize; this project is small and solo right now. If scope grows enough to justify it, add it back modeled on that repo's version rather than improvising one. **Rules out:** treating this repo's process docs as needing the same weight as a mature project's — they should stay this light until the project's actual complexity demands more.

## 2026-09-23 — Documentation-only commits allowed straight to `main`; everything else needs a branch

**Decided**, matching `extraction_project`'s convention. **Why:** cheap for the common case (a doc fix shouldn't need branch ceremony) while still keeping `main` buildable — application code always goes through review-by-self on a branch first. **Rules out:** committing a docs change bundled with app code straight to `main`; if they're bundled, the whole commit goes through a branch.

## 2026-09-23 — Tech stack taken as given from the source specification, not re-litigated

**Decided.** The technical stack and architecture (Node.js + TypeScript backend, React + TypeScript frontend, Socket.IO, HTML5 Canvas for 2D, Three.js/WebGL for 3D, npm-workspace `client`/`server`/`shared` layout) came from a design specification handed to this session (preserved in [engineering/architecture.md](engineering/architecture.md)) and is treated as the starting point rather than something to evaluate alternatives for.
